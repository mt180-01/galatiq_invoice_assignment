# Scanned invoices, end to end

Generated by `python scripts/evaluate_scans.py`; do not edit by hand.

**30 documents** from the independent extraction corpus (see [EXTRACTION_EVAL.md](EXTRACTION_EVAL.md)), each typeset onto a page, rasterised at 200 dpi and degraded like a flatbed scan (uneven lighting, sensor noise), so the only text is what Tesseract can recover. Then two readers, compared with the truth the corpus was built from:

* **rules**: the regex reader on the OCR text.
* **with model**: the pipeline's Intake: the model reads the OCR text (the rules are always in doubt on OCR) and is cross-checked.

Scanned invoices are never auto-paid (a person verifies OCR output), so the question is how much typing the person is saved.

**Reading this.** Two things decide how well a scan reads, and the first is not the model. (1) **OCR resolution.** Pages were first OCR'd at a fixed 200 dpi, where small type (9 pt) came out as garbage and the model could not repair what OCR had destroyed: on this set that gave 37% of scans fully right with the rules and 43% with the model. Tesseract's own confidence turned out to track accuracy closely (mean 57 at 200 dpi for 9 pt text, 95 at 300 dpi), so the page is now re-read at 300 and 400 dpi when confidence is low, and the most confident reading kept; that alone took the rules to the figure above. (2) **The model**, which then adds the rest, mostly on the line items (multi-column tables are what OCR scrambles). Whatever the reader, a scanned invoice is held for a person; the value of a good reading is a pre-filled form to check instead of a blank one.

Limits: typeset-then-degraded pages (one font family, flat), one OCR engine, one model, one recording: this is not a photograph of a crumpled receipt, and 30 documents shows patterns, not a precise rate.
